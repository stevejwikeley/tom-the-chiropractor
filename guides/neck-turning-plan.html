Your 12 Week Neck Turning Plan

A twelve week exercise plan for a stiff neck

Written by Tom Wikeley, chiropractor, registered with the General Chiropractic Council (No. 05268). Last reviewed November 2026.

Please read this first

This is an exercise plan I give to my own patients after I have examined them, for necks that have got stiff rather than necks that have just been injured. It is on the website so it is easy to find and re-read, and so anyone can see what a sensible plan looks like. It is general information, not a diagnosis, and not a treatment plan for anybody I have not assessed.

No plan can tell you what is causing your stiffness. That takes a proper history and examination, and some causes of a stiff neck need something other than exercises. If nobody has examined you, please treat this as background reading and see your GP, a chiropractor, a physiotherapist or another qualified clinician about your own symptoms. If a clinician who has examined you has given you different advice, follow theirs, not this.

Some symptoms need urgent care, not reading

Call 999 or go to A&E now if you have any of these, whatever else this guide says:

  • Numbness or tingling around your back passage, genitals or inner thighs
  • Difficulty passing urine, or losing control of your bladder or bowels
  • Sudden or worsening weakness in an arm or a leg
  • Chest pain or breathlessness, or a sudden severe headache unlike any you have had

Contact your GP or NHS 111 the same day if your pain comes with a fever, unexplained weight loss, or followed a significant fall or accident, or if you have a history of cancer.

This plan helps your neck turn more easily, so things like looking over your shoulder when driving feel easier. Your neck has got stiff over time, and gentle regular movement is the best way to loosen it. Progress is slow but steady, so stick with it.

Twelve weeks sounds a long time, and it is deliberate. Range that has been lost over years does not come back in a fortnight, and the people who get the most out of this are the ones who do a small amount almost every day rather than a lot in bursts. You should notice something before twelve weeks. The full stretch is there so the gains hold.

The plan starts with two exercises and adds one every four weeks. You keep the earlier ones going as you add the new ones, so by weeks 9 to 12 you are doing four.

How it should feel

  • A gentle stretch or mild ache is fine and normal.
  • Any ache should settle within an hour.
  • Move slowly. Never jerk or force your neck.
  • If an exercise makes things worse the next day, do fewer and tell me at your next visit.

Your plan at a glance

Weeks Exercises How often
1 to 4 Head turns, upper back twist Every day
5 to 8 Add chin tucks Every day
9 to 12 Add shoulder squeezes Every day

Keep doing the earlier exercises as you add new ones.

Weeks 1 to 4

1. Seated head turns

  • Sit tall in a firm chair, feet flat on the floor.
  • Slowly turn your head to the right as far as is comfortable.
  • Hold for 3 seconds, then come back to the middle.
  • Do the same to the left.
  • 10 turns each side, 3 times a day.

2. Seated upper back twist

  • Sit tall and cross your arms over your chest.
  • Slowly turn your shoulders and head together to the right, as far as is comfortable.
  • Hold for 3 seconds, then come back to the middle.
  • Do the same to the left.
  • 10 turns each side, 2 times a day.

Weeks 5 to 8

Keep doing both exercises above, but now hold each turn for 5 seconds.

3. Chin tucks

  • Sit tall and look straight ahead.
  • Gently slide your chin straight back, as if making a double chin. Keep your eyes level.
  • Hold for 5 seconds, then relax.
  • 10 times, 2 times a day.

Weeks 9 to 12

Keep doing all three exercises.

Head turns get a little help: when you reach the end of your turn, place one hand on your chin and gently ease it a little further. Hold for 5 seconds. Only ever a gentle push.

4. Shoulder squeezes

  • Sit or stand tall with your arms by your sides.
  • Gently squeeze your shoulder blades back and down, as if putting them in your back pockets.
  • Hold for 5 seconds, then relax.
  • 10 times, 2 times a day.

Check your progress

Sit in the same chair, facing the same way. Turn your head and notice what you can see at the edge of your view on each side, such as a door frame or picture. Check this in week 1, week 4, week 8 and week 12. Most people can see a little further each time.

Write it down somewhere, even on the back of this page. Memory is a poor judge of slow change, and a landmark you noted eight weeks ago is far more convincing than how you think your neck feels today.

Other things that help

  • A warm wheat bag or hot water bottle on your neck for 10 to 15 minutes before your exercises.
  • Short walks and staying generally active.
  • Doing your head turns little and often is better than lots all at once.

Stop and contact me if you notice

  • Dizziness, blurred or double vision
  • Trouble speaking or swallowing
  • New pain, pins and needles or numbness down your arm
  • Pain that is getting steadily worse

If any of these come on suddenly or feel severe, call 999 or go to A&E.

Anything else, including a few sore days, is worth mentioning at your next appointment rather than stopping the plan over.

Bring this with you when you come in, along with the landmarks you noted. We can adjust the numbers to suit how you are getting on.
