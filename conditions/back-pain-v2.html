What I treat

Back pain

Does this sound like you?

  • You bent down to pick something up and something "went." Now you can barely stand up straight
  • A dull ache that's been grumbling for months and you've stopped noticing how much you've stopped doing
  • It hurts most after sitting, and eases once you get moving
  • You're stiff and sore first thing in the morning
  • It flares up predictably, like after gardening, a long drive, or a heavy week

What's usually going on

Backs are strong. They're built to bend, lift and twist.

Most back pain isn't caused by damage. It's caused by the tissues of the back being loaded in a way they weren't ready for, too much, too suddenly, or the same thing over and over. The muscles around the area then tighten up protectively, which is why it can feel so alarming and so stuck.

Two things worth knowing

"Slipped disc" isn't really a thing. Discs don't slip. They can bulge or become irritated, and that can be painful, but the phrase makes it sound far more permanent than it is.

Most people don't need a scan. Scans of pain-free backs routinely show bulges, wear and degeneration. Because scan findings often don't match symptoms, imaging usually doesn't change what we'd do, which is why it's not the starting point.

How I can help

I treat general, acute and chronic back pain, whether or not it followed an injury or accident.

I'll work out what's driving it, treat the area hands-on, and give you a clear plan for getting back to normal activity. Often this will involve rehab, and I'll teach you more about pain to help you understand it.

What happens at your first appointment

I'll ask how it started and what makes it better or worse. Then I'll examine how your back moves, test the relevant nerves and muscles, and check the hips and mid-back, which often play a part.

You'll leave knowing what I think the problem is, in plain English.

How long will it take?

An acute lock-up often settles quickly. Many people are noticeably better within 1-4 visits. Long-standing back pain usually takes longer, around 4-10 appointments over several weeks.

Should I rest or keep moving?

Keep moving, within reason.

Bed rest was standard advice decades ago and it made people worse. Gentle, regular movement is now the clear recommendation. You don't need to push into severe pain, but you do need to keep the area moving.

When I'm not the right person

Go to A&E immediately

Go to A&E straight away if you have:

  • Numbness or tingling around your back passage, genitals or inner thighs
  • Difficulty passing urine, or loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Weakness in both legs

See your GP

See your GP first if your back pain started after a significant fall or accident, or comes with:

  • Fever
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • A history of cancer

What you can do right now

  • Walk. Little and often, even if it's five minutes.
  • Don't wrap yourself in cotton wool. Movement is treatment.
  • Change position regularly rather than finding one "safe" position and staying there.
