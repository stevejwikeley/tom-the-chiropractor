What I treat

Neck pain

Does this sound like you?

  • You wake up and can't turn your head to one side, it came on overnight, out of nowhere
  • Your neck is fine first thing, but aches from mid-afternoon onwards
  • You get a tight, gripping ache at the base of your skull
  • Checking your blind spot or reversing the car has become a whole-body manoeuvre
  • You've noticed pins and needles or a dull ache spreading into your shoulder or arm

What's usually going on

Most neck pain is mechanical. That means the problem is in how the joints, muscles and other tissues of the neck are moving and loading, not a disease, and not something sinister.

Your neck holds your head up all day. If you spend hours at a desk, in a van, or looking down at a phone, some structures end up doing far more work than others. Over time, that shows up as stiffness, aching and loss of movement.

The good news: mechanical problems respond well to being assessed properly and treated.

How I can help

I treat mechanical neck pain. That starts with working out which structures are actually involved, using a physical examination and a proper history.

Treatment usually combines hands-on work to the joints and soft tissues with specific advice about movement, work setup and loading.

We talk through what's going on and how it started. I examine your neck, movement, strength, and the surrounding areas that often contribute. Then I tell you plainly what I think is going on and what I'd suggest doing about it.

In most cases I'll treat you the same visit. If I don't think chiropractic is the right answer, I'll say so and point you somewhere better.

How long will it take?

Most people notice a change within 2-4 visits. A typical course is around 4-8 appointments, usually once per week unless the severity warrants a higher frequency. At that point we reassess together against your goals, some people are ready to finish up, others want to keep building on their progress beyond just being pain-free.

I'll give you an honest estimate at the end of your first appointment, and I'll tell you if it's not going the way I expected.

When I'm not the right person

Please see your GP, or go to A&E, rather than booking with me, if:

  • Your neck pain started after a car accident, fall or blow to the head
  • You have a fever, feel generally unwell, or have unexplained weight loss
  • You have weakness in your arm or hand, or you're dropping things
  • You have problems with balance, speech, swallowing or vision

Neck pain after an accident or injury needs medical assessment first.

What you can do right now

  • Keep moving. Gentle movement is best, as long as it isn't increasing your pain.
  • Change position often. Every 30 minutes at a desk, do something different for 30 seconds.
  • Check your pillow. One that keeps your head in line with your body, not propped up or drooping.
