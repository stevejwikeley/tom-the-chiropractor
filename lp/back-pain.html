What's usually going on

Backs are strong. They're built to bend, lift and twist. Most episodes of back pain do not mean your back is seriously damaged. The muscles around the area tighten up protectively, which is why it can feel so alarming and so stuck.

How I can help

I treat general, acute and chronic back pain. I'll work out what's driving it, treat the area hands-on, and give you a clear plan for getting back to normal activity. Often this will involve rehab, and I'll teach you more about pain to help you understand it.

When I'm not the right person

Go to A&E immediately

Go to A&E straight away if you have:

  • Numbness or tingling around your back passage, genitals or inner thighs
  • Difficulty passing urine, or loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Weakness in both legs

See your GP

See your GP first if your back pain started after a significant fall or accident, or comes with:

  • Fever
  • Unexplained weight loss
  • A history of cancer

Questions people ask me

What happens at my first appointment?

I'll take a full history, examine how you move, and explain what I think is going on in plain English. Many patients get some treatment on the same visit too.

Will it hurt?

Honestly, parts of it can be uncomfortable. Where muscles have tightened up protectively, working through them can bring tenderness in the muscle and the structures around it. That's temporary, and easing those areas is usually what starts to settle things down. Joint work tends to be easier, and some people feel relief straight away. Afterwards it's normal to feel a bit achy for a day or so, much like after exercise.

What's the clicking noise?

That sound is gas releasing from within the joint, the same thing that happens when you crack your knuckles. It isn't bone, and it isn't a sign of damage. If you'd rather avoid that technique altogether, just say so and I'll use something else. There's usually more than one way to treat a problem.

How many appointments will I need?

It varies from person to person, so I won't promise a number upfront. I'll give you a realistic estimate once I've assessed you, and there's no pressure to keep booking beyond what's actually helping.

Do I need a GP referral?

No GP referral needed. Book online in under a minute, or by WhatsApp. I'm Tom Wikeley, MChiro, registered with the General Chiropractic Council (No. 05268) and fully insured, and you'll see me at every appointment.
