What's usually going on

Most of the pain golfers bring me is mechanical. That means the problem is in how the joints, muscles and other tissues are moving and loading, not a disease, and not something sinister. A golf swing asks one side of your body to rotate hard and then stop hard, several dozen times in a round, and it does that on top of whatever your week already asks of you at a desk, in a van or on your feet. When some structures end up doing far more of that work than others, it shows up as stiffness, aching and lost movement.

How I can help

I treat mechanical pain in backs, necks, hips and shoulders. That starts with working out which structures are actually involved, using a physical examination and a proper history, including what your round and your week look like. Treatment usually combines hands-on work to the joints and soft tissues with specific advice about movement and loading.

To be straight with you: I'm not a golf specialist and I don't coach swings. If what you want is a swing fix, your pro is the right person. If something hurts, during a round or afterwards, that part I can help with.

When I'm not the right person

Please see your GP, or go to A&E, rather than booking with me, if:

  • Your pain started with a fall, a collision or a blow to the head
  • You felt a sudden tear or pop and the area is badly swollen, or you can't put weight through it
  • You have a fever, feel generally unwell, or have unexplained weight loss
  • You have weakness in an arm, hand or leg, or you're dropping things
  • You have chest pain or tightness, or pain that comes on with exertion and eases with rest
  • You have numbness around the saddle area, or any change in bladder or bowel control

Pain after an accident or injury needs medical assessment first, and anything that behaves like a heart problem needs urgent attention rather than an appointment with me.

Questions people ask me

Will I have to stop playing?

Usually not altogether. More often it's a question of changing something about how much you play, or how you warm up, for a while. I'd rather work out what your body will tolerate than hand you a blanket ban, and I'll tell you honestly if resting it really is the sensible option for a few weeks.

What happens at my first appointment?

I'll take a full history, examine how you move, and explain what I think is going on in plain English. Many patients get some treatment on the same visit too.

Will it hurt?

Honestly, parts of it can be uncomfortable. Where muscles have tightened up protectively, working through them can bring tenderness in the muscle and the structures around it. That's temporary, and easing those areas is usually what starts to settle things down. Joint work tends to be easier, and some people feel relief straight away. Afterwards it's normal to feel a bit achy for a day or so, much like after exercise.

What's the clicking noise?

That sound is gas releasing from within the joint, the same thing that happens when you crack your knuckles. It isn't bone, and it isn't a sign of damage. If you'd rather avoid that technique altogether, just say so and I'll use something else. There's usually more than one way to treat a problem.

How many appointments will I need?

It varies from person to person, so I won't promise a number upfront. I'll give you a realistic estimate once I've assessed you, and there's no pressure to keep booking beyond what's actually helping.

Do I need a GP referral?

No GP referral needed. Book online in under a minute, or by WhatsApp. I'm Tom Wikeley, MChiro, registered with the General Chiropractic Council (No. 05268) and fully insured, and you'll see me at every appointment.
